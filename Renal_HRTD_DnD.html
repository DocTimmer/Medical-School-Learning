The Breakdown: The Renal Tubulopathies

1. Bartter Syndrome (The Loop Mimic)

Bartter syndrome knocks out the NKCC2 transporter in the Thick Ascending Limb (TAL), functioning exactly as if the patient were on a continuous Loop Diuretic. Because the TAL is responsible for establishing the medullary concentration gradient and driving paracellular calcium/magnesium reabsorption, patients waste massive amounts of salt and water (polyuria, dehydration). The loss of the positive lumen potential means calcium is dumped into the urine (Hypercalciuria). The volume loss triggers a massive RAAS response (High Renin, High Aldosterone), which drives potassium out into the urine (Hypokalemia), yet blood pressure remains normal to low due to the fluid wasting.

2. Gitelman Syndrome (The Thiazide Mimic)

Gitelman syndrome knocks out the NCC transporter in the Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT), functioning exactly like a Thiazide Diuretic. Unlike the TAL, the DCT actually increases calcium reabsorption when sodium is blocked. Therefore, Gitelman patients have Hypocalciuria (a major distinguishing factor from Bartter). They also uniquely waste magnesium, presenting with severe Hypomagnesemia and muscle cramps. Like Bartter, they waste volume, triggering RAAS (High Renin/Aldo) resulting in Hypokalemia and normal/low BP.

3. Liddle Syndrome (The Aldosterone Imposter)

Liddle syndrome is a gain-of-function mutation causing ENaC overactivity in the Collecting Duct. The body is constantly reabsorbing sodium and water, mimicking an aldosterone-secreting tumor, leading to early, severe Hypertension. However, because the body is incredibly volume-overloaded, it completely shuts down the RAAS system. Thus, it presents with Low Renin and Low Aldosterone. The rampant sodium reabsorption forces potassium out, leading to Hypokalemia despite the suppressed RAAS.
