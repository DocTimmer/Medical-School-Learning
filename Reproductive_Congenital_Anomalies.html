The Breakdown: GU & Reproductive Anomalies

1. Müllerian (Paramesonephric) Duct Anomalies

The paramesonephric ducts must fuse to form the normal uterus. If they fail to fuse completely, you get Uterus Didelphys (double uterus, double cervix). If they partially fail to fuse, you get a Bicornuate Uterus (heart-shaped, two horns sharing a lower uterus). If they fuse correctly but the septum between them fails to dissolve, you get a Septate Uterus (one uterus, internal wall), which is a classic cause of recurrent miscarriages.

2. Urethral Opening Defects (Epispadias vs. Hypospadias)

Remember the directional associations: Hypospadias is a ventral opening caused by the failure of the urethral folds to fuse (often seen with a hooded foreskin). Epispadias is a dorsal opening caused by the faulty positioning of the genital tubercle. Epispadias is heavily associated with bladder exstrophy!

3. Vaginal & Hymenal Anomalies

Vaginal atresia occurs when the urogenital sinus fails to form the lower portion of the vagina, leaving only a dimple but an intact upper reproductive tract. Imperforate hymen is similar in presentation (primary amenorrhea, pelvic pain) but features a classic bulging, bluish membrane caused by trapped menstrual blood (hematocolpos).

4. The Remnants (Gartner Cyst & Hydroceles)

In females, the Wolffian (mesonephric) duct is supposed to degenerate. If it persists, it forms a fluid-filled Gartner Cyst along the lateral vaginal wall. In males, the processus vaginalis brings the testes down into the scrotum. If it doesn't close up, peritoneal fluid leaks down, causing a Communicating Hydrocele.
