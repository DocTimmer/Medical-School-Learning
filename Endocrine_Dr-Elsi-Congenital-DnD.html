Endocrine Embryology & Pathology Review

Connecting the embryologic germ layer origin directly explains congenital abnormalities and neuroendocrine tumors:

1. Pituitary Gland: Dual Origin

Anterior Pituitary (Adenohypophysis): Derived from Oral Ectoderm via Rathke pouch. Remnants of Rathke pouch can calcify and give rise to Craniopharyngiomas (cystic suprasellar mass with 'motor oil' fluid). Secretes FSH, LH, ACTH, TSH, PRL, GH, and β-endorphin.

Posterior Pituitary (Neurohypophysis): Derived from Neuroectoderm (infundibulum from the diencephalon). Stores and releases Oxytocin and ADH synthesized in the hypothalamus. Pathology includes Central Diabetes Insipidus and SIADH.

2. Thyroid & Parathyroid Glands

Thyroid: Follicular cells originate from the Endoderm (floor of primitive pharynx descending via the thyroglossal duct). Persistent remnants lead to thyroglossal duct cysts (midline, moves up with swallowing) or lingual thyroid. Parafollicular (C cells) derive from Neural Crest.

Parathyroid: Derived from the Endoderm of the 3rd (inferior) and 4th (superior) pharyngeal pouches. Defective 3rd/4th pouch development in DiGeorge Syndrome (22q11.2 deletion) causes congenital absence of parathyroids (hypocalcemia) and thymus (T-cell immunodeficiency).

3. Adrenal Gland: Dual Origin

Adrenal Cortex: Derived from Mesoderm. Synthesizes steroid hormones: Aldosterone (GFR - Glomerulosa), Cortisol (Fasciculata), and Androgens/DHEA (Reticularis). Defects in steroidogenesis cause Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH).

Adrenal Medulla: Derived from Neural Crest cells (Chromaffin cells). Synthesizes catecholamines (Epinephrine, Norepinephrine). Tumors include adult Pheochromocytoma (rule of 10s, episodic headaches/palpitations/sweating) and pediatric Neuroblastoma (N-myc amplification, opsoclonus-myoclonus).
