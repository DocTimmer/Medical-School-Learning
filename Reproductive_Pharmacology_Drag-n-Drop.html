GnRH AnalogLeuprolide GnRH analog:
• Pulsatile fashion: Increases FSH and LH (stimulates pituitary gonadotropes).
• Continuous fashion: Inhibits FSH and LH (downregulates GnRH receptors → chemical castration / hypogonadism).		 • Pulsatile: Treatment of infertility (induces LH surge & ovulation).
• Continuous: Treatment of prostate cancer, uterine fibroids (leiomyomas), endometriosis, and precocious puberty.		 • Nausea, vomiting.
• Transient tumor flare during first 1–2 weeks of continuous use.
• Antiandrogen/hypoestrogenic effects: hot flashes, bone loss / osteoporosis, decreased libido, erectile dysfunction.		 Pulsatile = Puberty / Pregnancy (stimulates LH/FSH); Continuous = Castration (suppresses LH/FSH). When initiating continuous therapy for metastatic prostate cancer, always co-administer an androgen receptor antagonist (e.g., flutamide) for the first 1–2 weeks to prevent tumor flare (bone pain, urinary obstruction) from the initial LH/testosterone spike.
5α-Reductase InhibitorFinasteride 5-α-reductase inhibitor: Competitively blocks the intracellular conversion of testosterone to the more potent androgen dihydrotestosterone (DHT) in the prostate, hair follicles, and peripheral tissues. • Treatment of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) (slowly reduces prostate size and urinary obstruction over 6–12 months).
• Treatment of male pattern baldness (androgenetic alopecia at lower doses).		 • Gynecomastia, erectile dysfunction, decreased libido, decreased ejaculate volume.
• Teratogenic: Category X (inhibits external virilization of male fetuses).		 Decreases baseline serum prostate-specific antigen (PSA) by ~50% (always double the measured PSA value when screening for prostate cancer). Teratogenic: Pregnant women must not even handle crushed tablets due to the risk of ambiguous genitalia / hypospadias in a male fetus.
Androgen Receptor BlockerFlutamide Competitive inhibitor at testosterone and DHT receptors: Nonsteroidal competitive antagonist at nuclear androgen receptors, blocking androgen uptake and androgen-driven gene transcription in prostatic tissue. Used in combination with continuous leuprolide (GnRH agonist) in the treatment of advanced / metastatic prostate cancer. • Gynecomastia, hot flashes, decreased libido, and impotence.
• Hepatotoxicity (black-box warning; requires baseline and periodic liver function tests).		 Key role: Prevents the dangerous initial 'tumor flare' (acute bone pain, spinal cord compression, bladder outlet obstruction) caused by the transient surge of LH and testosterone during the first 1–2 weeks of continuous leuprolide therapy.
Aldosterone & Androgen BlockerSpironolactone Blocks testosterone receptors and inhibits 17α-hydroxylase / 17,20-lyase (suppresses androgen synthesis); also acts as a potassium-sparing diuretic by competitively blocking aldosterone receptors in the renal cortical collecting tubule. • Used to treat hirsutism and acne in PCOS (reduces peripheral androgenic effects).
• Also used in hypertension, congestive heart failure (reduces mortality in HFrEF), and ascites in hepatic cirrhosis.		 • Gynecomastia, amenorrhea / menstrual irregularities (due to progesterone receptor binding).
• Hyperkalemia (blocks aldosterone-mediated K+ secretion).		 Classic cause of drug-induced gynecomastia (mnemonic: 'Some Drugs Create Awesome Knockers' — Spironolactone, Digoxin, Cimetidine, Alcohol, Ketoconazole). If gynecomastia or menstrual side effects develop, switch to eplerenone (selective aldosterone antagonist with no antiandrogenic effects).
SERM / Ovulation InducerClomiphene Partial agonist / antagonist at estrogen receptors in the hypothalamus: Prevents estrogen negative feedback → increases pulsatile GnRH release → leads to increased LH and FSH secretion, which induces follicular growth and triggers ovulation. First-line agent to promote ovulation in PCOS and anovulatory infertility in women desiring pregnancy. • Hot flashes, abdominal discomfort.
• Multiple pregnancies (twins/triplets due to multiple follicular maturation).
• Visual disturbances (scotomas/blurring) and ovarian enlargement.		 Vignette clue: Infertility in a woman with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Clomiphene tricks the hypothalamic-pituitary axis into sensing hypoestrogenism, sparking endogenous gonadotropin release. Letrozole (aromatase inhibitor) is now also widely used with high live-birth rates.
SERM (Breast Antagonist / Endometrial Agonist)Tamoxifen Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM):
• Estrogen receptor antagonist at the breast.
• Partial estrogen receptor agonist on endometrial tissue and bone.		 Treatment and prevention of ER-positive (and PR-positive) breast cancer in both premenopausal and postmenopausal women. • Increased risk of endometrial hyperplasia and endometrial cancer (agonist at uterus).
• Deep venous thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) (hepatic procoagulant synthesis).
• Hot flashes.		 Board classic: Any postmenopausal bleeding or abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB) in a patient on tamoxifen demands prompt endometrial biopsy to rule out endometrial carcinoma. Agonist action at bone preserves bone mineral density.
SERM (Bone Agonist / Endometrial Antagonist)Raloxifene Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM):
• Estrogen receptor agonist at bone (inhibits osteoclast resorption).
• Estrogen receptor antagonist at breast AND endometrium.		 Treatment and prevention of postmenopausal osteoporosis; also reduces risk of invasive breast cancer in high-risk postmenopausal women. • Leg cramps, hot flashes.
• Deep venous thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) (increased thromboembolism risk).		 Mnemonic: 'Raloxifene = Relax in the endometrium'. Because it is an antagonist at endometrial tissue, it carries NO increased risk of endometrial cancer! Favored over tamoxifen when treating postmenopausal osteoporosis, especially in women with intact uteri.
Aromatase InhibitorAnastrozole Aromatase inhibitor: Competitively inhibits the CYP19A1 aromatase enzyme, preventing the peripheral conversion of androstenedione to estrone and testosterone to estradiol in adipose tissue. First-line adjuvant treatment of ER-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women (also letrozole and exemestane). • Osteoporosis / bone fractures (due to profound systemic hypoestrogenism; monitor with baseline DEXA scans).
• Arthralgias / joint pain, hot flashes, vaginal dryness.		 Only effective in postmenopausal women where estrogen production depends on peripheral aromatization of adrenal androgens. In premenopausal women, ovarian aromatase would easily overcome competitive inhibition through compensatory gonadotropin release.
Progesterone Receptor AntagonistMifepristone (RU-486) Competitive progesterone receptor antagonist: Competitively blocks progesterone receptors PR-A and PR-B, depriving the endometrium of progestational support; also acts as a glucocorticoid receptor antagonist at high doses. • Elective medical abortion (administered in combination with misoprostol through 10 weeks / 70 days gestation).
• Emergency postcoital contraception; hyperglycemia in refractory Cushing syndrome.		 • Heavy vaginal bleeding, severe abdominal pain / uterine cramping, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, risk of incomplete abortion. Medical abortion 1–2 punch: Mifepristone (blocks progesterone → decidual necrosis, blastocyst detachment, sensitizes myometrium) followed 24–48 hours later by Misoprostol (PGE1 analog → induces strong uterine contractions to expel products of conception).
Combined Oral ContraceptiveOral Contraceptives Prevents estrogen surge and thus the LH surge by maintaining steady negative feedback levels of estrogen and progesterone → inhibits ovulation. Progestins also thicken cervical mucus (blocks sperm) and thin the endometrium. • Contraception (does not protect against STDs).
• Regulate irregular or painful menses (dysmenorrhea/menorrhagia), endometriosis, acne.
• Protective against ovarian and endometrial cancer.		 • Depression, weight gain, hypertension, deep venous thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), hepatic adenomas.
• Contraindicated in women >35 years who smoke or have a history of thromboembolic disease, stroke, migraine with aura, or estrogen-sensitive tumors.		 Smoking + age >35 is an absolute contraindication due to high cardiovascular / myocardial infarction / stroke risk. Long-term use reduces the risk of epithelial ovarian cancer and endometrial cancer by >50%, but slightly increases the risk of benign hepatic adenomas.
Prostaglandin E2 AnalogDinoprostone Prostaglandin E₂ analog: Binds EP prostaglandin receptors to stimulate collagenase breakdown and increase water content in cervical stroma (cervical ripening); also stimulates rhythmic myometrial smooth muscle contractions. • Makes the cervix favorable and induces labor (ripens/dilates cervix and promotes uterine contractions at term).
• Can also be used as an abortive therapy (evacuation of uterine contents in fetal demise).		 • Nausea, abdominal pain / uterine cramping, diarrhea, fever.
• Uterine hyperstimulation (tachysystole) and uterine rupture.		 Dinoprostone = PGE2 (cervical ripening / labor induction). Contrast with Misoprostol = PGE1 (abortifacient/labor induction) and Carboprost = PGF2α (uterine atony / postpartum hemorrhage; contraindicated in asthma). Contraindicated in patients with prior classical cesarean delivery due to uterine rupture risk.
β2-Adrenergic Agonist (Tocolytic)Ritodrine / Terbutaline β₂-agonists at uterine smooth muscle: Stimulates β2-adrenergic receptors → ↑ intracellular cAMP → inhibits myosin light-chain kinase (MLCK) → relaxes myometrial smooth muscle. Tocolysis: Reduces uterine contractions to arrest preterm labor and prolong pregnancy for up to 48 hours. • Tachycardia (both maternal and fetal reflex/direct tachycardia), palpitations, tremors.
• Maternal hyperglycemia, hypokalemia, and pulmonary edema.		 Mnemonic: 'Ritodrine / Terbutaline Returns fetus to Dreams' (delays delivery). Used to buy 48 hours to administer antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone or dexamethasone) to accelerate fetal lung maturation and surfactant synthesis via type II pneumocytes, preventing neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (NRDS).
α1-Adrenergic AntagonistTerazosin / Tamsulosin α₁-adrenergic antagonist: Relaxes smooth muscle in the prostatic stroma, bladder neck, and capsule.
• Terazosin: Nonselective α₁A, α₁B, and α₁D antagonist.
• Tamsulosin: Selective for α₁A,D receptors found predominantly in the prostate.		 • Treatment of BPH if urinary symptoms are present (provides rapid relief of lower urinary tract symptoms within days).
• Terazosin also treats concurrent hypertension.		 • Hypotension (especially terazosin due to vascular α1B blockade; first-dose orthostatic syncope/dizziness).
• Tamsulosin causes significantly less hypotension, but can cause retrograde ejaculation and intraoperative floppy iris syndrome (IFIS) during cataract surgery.		 Board Decision: Patient with BPH + Hypertension → Choose terazosin or doxazosin. Patient with BPH + Normotensive → Choose tamsulosin (prostate-selective, avoids hypotension). Patients on tamsulosin must alert their ophthalmologist prior to cataract surgery due to floppy iris risk.
PDE-5 InhibitorSildenafil, Vardenafil, Tadalafil Increases cGMP by inhibiting its breakdown by phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5): Causes prolonged nitric oxide-mediated vascular smooth muscle relaxation in the corpus cavernosum and pulmonary vascular bed → increased blood flow leads to erection. • Treatment of erectile dysfunction (ED).
• Can also be used to treat Raynaud syndrome or pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) (sildenafil = Revatio).
• Tadalafil also approved for BPH.		 • Headache and facial flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion.
• Cyanopsia (blue-tinted vision) due to retinal PDE-6 inhibition.
• Life-threatening hypotension in patients taking nitroglycerin / nitrates.		 Lethal Contraindication with Nitrates: Nitrates stimulate guanylyl cyclase (↑ cGMP synthesis) while PDE-5 inhibitors block cGMP degradation → massive synergistic intracellular cGMP accumulation → profound refractory vasodilation, severe hypotension, and death! Must separate nitrates and PDE-5 inhibitors by at least 24 hours (48 hours for tadalafil).
