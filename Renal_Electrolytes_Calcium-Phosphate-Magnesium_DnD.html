The Breakdown: Electrolyte Physiology

1. Calcium vs. Phosphate (The Seesaw)

Calcium and Phosphate have an inverse relationship driven primarily by PTH. PTH raises Calcium and lowers Phosphate (by dumping it into the urine). Hypercalcemia causes "stones, bones, groans, and psychiatric overtones" with a shortened QT interval. Hypocalcemia causes neuromuscular excitability (Tetany, Chvostek/Trousseau signs) with a prolonged QT interval.

2. The Refeeding Syndrome Trap

Notice that "Refeeding syndrome" and "Insulin therapy in DKA" both cause Hypophosphatemia. Why? Because insulin aggressively pulls phosphate (along with glucose and potassium) into the cells to make ATP. If a starved patient gets suddenly fed, insulin spikes, blood phosphate drops to near zero, and the body can't make ATP—leading to severe muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis) and respiratory failure.

3. Magnesium: The Gatekeeper

Hypomagnesemia is infamous on the boards for one major reason: You cannot correct Hypokalemia if Magnesium is low! Magnesium normally plugs the ROMK channels in the kidney to prevent potassium from leaking out. If Mg is low, the plug is gone, and potassium pours into the urine. Therefore, low Mg leads to refractory hypokalemia and hypocalcemia.

Conversely, Hypermagnesemia (often given intentionally as Mg-Sulfate to prevent seizures in preeclampsia) acts as a calcium channel blocker. The classic toxicity sign is the loss of Deep Tendon Reflexes (DTRs).
